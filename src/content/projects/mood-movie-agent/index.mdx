## The Problem

Choosing what to watch shouldn't feel like a chore. Traditional recommendation systems rely on viewing history and ratings — but what you want to watch on a rainy Sunday afternoon is fundamentally different from what you'd enjoy on a Friday night. Mood drives entertainment choices far more than past behavior.

## Approach

Designed a conversational agent that understands emotional context through natural dialogue. Instead of browsing genres or scrolling endlessly, users simply describe how they feel — and the agent surfaces thoughtful, personalized recommendations.

### Key Design Decisions

- **Conversational Interface**: Natural language input rather than dropdowns or filters. Users describe their mood in their own words.
- **Emotional Mapping**: Developed a nuanced emotion-to-genre mapping system that goes beyond simple sentiment analysis to understand complex emotional states.
- **Curated Quality**: The agent prioritizes quality over quantity — recommending 3–5 carefully selected films rather than overwhelming lists.

## Architecture

- **Frontend**: Next.js application with a clean conversational UI, streaming responses, and elegant movie cards
- **AI Layer**: OpenAI GPT model with carefully crafted system prompts for emotional understanding and movie knowledge
- **Data**: Integration with movie databases for metadata, ratings, availability, and poster imagery
- **Agent Logic**: Multi-step reasoning — mood extraction → genre mapping → filtering → ranking → presentation

## Learnings

1. **Prompt engineering is product design** — The quality of recommendations is directly tied to how well the system prompt frames the agent's personality, constraints, and reasoning approach.
2. **Less is more in recommendations** — Users respond better to 3 thoughtful suggestions than 20 algorithmically generated ones.
3. **Conversation design matters** — The way the agent asks follow-up questions and presents results is as important as the underlying model quality.
